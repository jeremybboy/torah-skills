# Chapter Methodology

Use this reference for full parashah chapters and for any artifact that needs the user's core Torah study method.

## Required 10-Step Framework

Every parashah chapter follows this order:

1. Opening Reflection
2. Narrative Overview
3. Five Principal Sugyot
4. Five Key Verses
5. Advanced Interpretation
6. Personal Application
7. Parenting Reflection
8. Dialectical Analysis
9. Cross-Disciplinary Connection
10. Calendar Integration

## Section Requirements

### 1. Opening Reflection

Start with orientation before interpretation. Include a framing question, several reflection prompts, and a central theme.

### 2. Narrative Overview

Identify main characters, historical setting, chronological flow, geographic context, and a one-sentence summary.

### 3. Five Principal Sugyot

Each sugya should include:

- Torah source
- Primary classical source, when available
- Additional classical sources
- Context
- Kushya: the interpretive or moral problem
- Terutz: how classical sources develop or resolve it
- Halachic implications, if relevant
- Philosophical implications
- Personal application
- Core takeaway

### 4. Five Key Verses

Each verse should include:

- Hebrew
- Transliteration
- Translation
- Peshat
- Commentary
- Why it matters

### 5. Advanced Interpretation

Separate subsections for Talmud and Midrash, Rishonim, Kabbalah and Chassidut, and halachic development when relevant. Do not let mystical or homiletical readings replace peshat.

### 6. Personal Application

Use practical, emotional, and spiritual lessons. Keep application downstream from the text and sources.

### 7. Parenting Reflection

Connect the parashah to fatherhood, parenting, leadership, emotional presence, responsibility, and role modeling only when the connection is textually grounded.

### 8. Dialectical Analysis

Use thesis, antithesis, synthesis, deeper tension, and systems thinking. The synthesis should clarify the parashah's internal tensions, not flatten them.

### 9. Cross-Disciplinary Connection

Choose one serious analogy, such as systems architecture, organizational learning, psychology, law, education, or leadership. Develop it deeply instead of adding decorative comparisons.

### 10. Calendar Integration

Identify the parashah's place in the annual Torah cycle, current or target-year timing, Israel vs. Diaspora differences when relevant, double-parashah considerations, holidays or fasts, haftarah context, and transition into the following parashah.

## Selection Rubric

Choose the five sugyot and five key verses by ranked learning value, not by whatever is merely interesting.

1. Canonical recurrence: prefer material that reappears across Torah, Tanach, Chazal, halachah, liturgy, or the calendar.
2. Source density: prefer material with sustained treatment in Rashi, Ramban, Sforno, Midrash, Gemara, Rambam, Shulchan Arukh, or major later works.
3. Interpretive pressure: prefer verses that create serious questions, including contradiction, repetition, moral tension, theological difficulty, or unusual wording.
4. Halachic or practical afterlife: prefer material that develops into Jewish practice, law, prayer, education, ethics, or communal structure.
5. Theological and philosophical weight: prefer verses that define God, covenant, human responsibility, leadership, memory, law, exile, return, justice, or love.
6. Calendar and liturgical relevance: prefer material tied to Shabbat Chazon, Shabbat Nachamu, holidays, fasts, haftarot, or recurring liturgical use.
7. Chapter balance: avoid five verses that all do the same job. A chapter should usually include narrative, law, theology, leadership, and application when the parashah supports that range.

## Editorial Rules

- Start with orientation before interpretation.
- Identify source categories clearly.
- Separate peshat from midrash, halachah, mysticism, and personal application.
- Use direct quotations sparingly.
- Do not invent certainty where sources disagree.
- Do not call AI output final until human review confirms verse selection, source quality, and commentary accuracy.
- Do not force weak material into the template; let the parashah's strongest learning value shape emphasis.

## Review Checklist

- All ten required sections are present.
- Five sugyot are present.
- Five key verses are present.
- Selection criteria are stated or clearly implied.
- Every major claim has a source anchor.
- Source links point to the intended text.
- Calendar and haftarah details are checked.
- Personal application follows from the text.
- Parenting reflection is grounded in the parashah.
- Cross-disciplinary material deepens one analogy.
- Missing themes and weak claims are marked for human review.

## Source Baseline

Each chapter should identify:

1. Primary Torah verse or passage.
2. Main classical sources.
3. Whether each major claim is peshat, Midrash, Gemara, Rishonim, Halachah, philosophy, Kabbalah, Chassidut, or application.
4. Any uncertainty or disputed interpretation.
5. Whether a calendar claim was checked against a current calendar source.
