# Selection Criteria

The five key verses and five sugyot should not be chosen because they are merely interesting. They should be chosen because they carry the parashah's strongest learning value.

## Ranked Rubric

1. Canonical recurrence  
   Prefer scenes, verses, or themes that reappear across Torah, Tanach, Chazal, halachah, liturgy, or the calendar.

2. Source density  
   Prefer material with sustained treatment in Rashi, Ramban, Sforno, Midrash, Gemara, Rambam, Shulchan Arukh, or major later works.

3. Interpretive pressure  
   Prefer verses that create serious questions: contradiction, repetition, moral tension, theological difficulty, or unusual wording.

4. Halachic or practical afterlife  
   Prefer material that develops into Jewish practice, law, prayer, education, ethics, or communal structure.

5. Theological and philosophical weight  
   Prefer verses that define God, covenant, human responsibility, leadership, memory, law, exile, return, justice, or love.

6. Calendar and liturgical relevance  
   Prefer material tied to Shabbat Chazon, Shabbat Nachamu, holidays, fasts, haftarot, or recurring liturgical use.

7. Chapter balance  
   Avoid five verses that all do the same job. A chapter should usually include narrative, law, theology, leadership, and application when the parashah supports that range.

## Editorial Rule

The chapter structure is fixed. The emphasis is not fixed. Each parashah should be treated with the same method, but not forced into the same thematic shape.
