# Chapter Template

When useful, begin with an optional **Step 0: Orientation Prelude**: locate the book and passage, larger narrative, people and relationships, relevant geography, genealogy, or history, festival or special-reading context, and a familiar Jewish-life entry point. Do not force a hook; Step 0 does not renumber or replace the required framework.

Every parashah chapter then follows this order.

1. Opening Reflection
2. Narrative Overview — include what precedes the parashah, its place in the larger narrative, and meaningful comparisons with repeated or retold accounts.
3. Five Principal Sugyot — select genuine tensions and develop each through source → category → context → kushya → classical responses/terutz → halachic implications when relevant → philosophical implications → personal implications and application → core idea.
4. Five Key Verses
5. Advanced Interpretation
6. Personal Application
7. Parenting Reflection
8. Dialectical Analysis
9. Cross-Disciplinary Connection
10. Calendar Integration

## Editorial Notes

- Start with orientation before interpretation.
- Give beginners proportionate context and define unfamiliar people, places, and source categories on first use.
- Identify source category clearly: Torah/Tanakh, Mishnah, Gemara/Talmud, Midrash, Rishonim, Halachah, Kabbalah, Chassidut, modern application.
- Separate peshat from midrash, halachah, mysticism, and personal application.
- For significant repetition, ask what repeats, what differs, why it matters, and what deeper literary or legal design the comparison reveals.
- Use direct quotations sparingly.
- Do not invent certainty where sources disagree.
- Treat AI output as draft material until human review confirms the source chain.
