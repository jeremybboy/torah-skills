Welcome to a short Torah Skills preview of Vayishlach. How do you approach someone you fear without denying danger or deciding in advance what that person will do?

Jacob hears that Esau is approaching with four hundred men. He divides his camp, prays, and sends gifts. Rashi hears two concerns: fear for his own life and distress that he could harm others. The verse itself does not spell out those motives.

At the Jabbok, Jacob struggles through the night. Genesis first describes his opponent as a man; the prophet Hosea speaks of an angel. Jacob leaves with a new name, Israel, and a limp. He has prevailed, and he is still hurt. The Torah links the injury to a food practice that later rabbinic discussion develops.

Esau runs to Jacob. They embrace and weep. Rashi preserves two readings of the kiss: one doubts that it was wholehearted; the other takes the compassion of that moment seriously. The brothers later go their own ways.

In Shechem, Dinah is violated. The narrator does not record her speaking. Ramban distinguishes a plan to bring her home from the wider revenge carried out by two of her brothers. Protection and retaliation are not the same thing.

Finally, Jacob returns to Bethel, where he once made a vow. He builds an altar and recalls the God who accompanied him. As you study, ask what it takes to honor a promise after the danger has passed.
